1. Purpose and how to read this document
This document states what GK Furnish must become next. PRD-001 describes the product as it is built today.
PRD-002 keeps what works, changes what slows customers and the owner down, and adds what is missing: manual
payment confirmation, a simpler home page, a personal shop page for signed-in customers, a one-tap order workflow,
messaging through WhatsApp and email, and safe delete and reset tools.
It is a product document, not a technical design. It says what the product must do and what rules it must keep. When
a later change conflicts with a requirement here, the change must update this document in the same piece of work.
Reading the requirement tables
  ID is a stable reference (for example PAY-7). Use it in tasks, tests and discussions.
 Change shows how the requirement relates to PRD-001: KEEP (unchanged), CHANGED (rewritten), NEW (did
not exist), REMOVED (no longer a requirement).
 The second line of the Change column is the release phase: P1, P2 or P3 (section 23).
 "Must" means required for the phase it is in. "Should" means expected unless there is a stated reason not to.

4. Product summary, goals and success measures
GK Furnish is the public shop and the private workshop office of a furniture maker in Mbeya. Customers browse
chairs, tables, beds, lamps and custom work, choose size, material and colour, and order for delivery or collection.
The owner runs products, orders, production, stock, suppliers, money and the words and pictures of the site from one
admin portal.
The shop serves a general audience: homes, students, schools, offices, hotels, guest houses, churches and other
organisations. It is not a student-only catalogue.
Positioning. Durable furniture, made by hand in Mbeya or with checked partner carpenters, at a price agreed before work starts.
Study chairs and study tables are made in the workshop. Beds and larger pieces may be made with partners and are checked
before they leave.
4.1 Goals
 1. A first-time visitor reaches a product in one tap from the home page.
2. A customer places an order without an account in under three minutes.
3. The customer knows exactly how to pay and tells the workshop they paid in under two minutes.
4. The owner confirms a payment and moves an order forward in two taps from the order screen.
5. The customer is told what is happening at each stage and has reasons to return.

 Staff roles below the owner. The portal is for the owner.
 Asking for a mobile-money PIN on the website, or treating a customer's claim of payment as money received.
 Automatic payment collection (PalmPesa) in this version. The code may stay behind a switch (PAY-20).
 The WhatsApp Business API, a WhatsApp inbox in the portal, SMS, card payments, and bulk marketing in the first
release.

8. Payments: manual confirmation
Principle. The customer pays outside the site, on their own phone or at a bank, using the owner's instructions. The site records
the customer's claim. Only the owner's confirmation adds money to an order. The website never asks for a mobile-money PIN.
8.1 Flow
 1. The customer places the order and lands on the pay page with the order number.
2. The pay page shows the amount due now and each enabled payment method: name, number or account,
account name, and copy buttons. The reference to use is the order number.
3. The customer pays on their phone or at the bank.
4. The customer taps "I have paid" and enters the method, amount, transaction ID, and optionally a screenshot and
the paying phone number.
5. The payment is stored as Submitted. The order shows "Awaiting confirmation". The customer sees the expected
confirmation time. The owner is alerted.
6. The owner checks the transaction ID and amount in their own mobile-money or bank statement, then taps
Confirm or Reject.
7. Confirm: the payment gets a receipt number, the balance updates, the order moves to Deposit confirmed if the
advance is reached, stock is reduced once, and the owner is offered the "Payment confirmed" message. Reject:
the customer sees the reason and can resubmit.

11. Messaging customers
The owner sends messages from the portal in two ways. WhatsApp click-to-chat opens WhatsApp with the
message ready; the owner taps Send. Email is sent by the server through an email service. WhatsApp is the main
channel because many customers give no email. Email suits automatic messages and anything that needs a record.
11.1 Limits of click-to-chat (to state plainly)
  The portal can open WhatsApp but cannot know whether the owner pressed Send. The log therefore says
"Opened in WhatsApp", not "Sent".
 It cannot attach photos or files. Production photos are shown on the order page and the message carries the
tracking link.
 Replies arrive in the owner's own WhatsApp, not in the portal. There is no inbox in the portal.
 It cannot send automatically. Automatic WhatsApp messages need the WhatsApp Business API, which is a later
phase (P3).

21. Main journeys
21.1 A guest buys a piece
 1. The visitor opens a product. Most chosen options are preselected. They see price, lead time and the deposit due
today.
2. They tap Buy now. Checkout step 1 asks name, phone and delivery details.
3. Step 2 shows totals and the plan. They tick the agreement and tap "Place order and see how to pay".
4. The pay page shows the order number, the amount due, the reservation notice and the payment methods with
copy buttons.
5. They pay on their phone, then tap "I have paid" and enter the transaction ID. They may tap "Tell us on
WhatsApp".
6. The order shows "Awaiting confirmation". They are offered to create an account to track and reorder.
21.2 The owner confirms and starts work
 1. The dashboard shows "1 payment to confirm". The owner taps it and checks the ID and amount in their own
statement.
2. The owner taps Confirm. The order moves to Deposit confirmed; stock reduces once; a receipt is created.
3. The Notify sheet opens with "Payment confirmed" prefilled. The owner taps WhatsApp, then Send in WhatsApp.
4. Later the owner marks specifications agreed, taps "Start production", and sends the production message.
21.3 Handover and follow-up
 1. The owner taps "Mark ready" and sends the Ready message with the balance due.
2. The customer pays the balance through the same pay page. The owner confirms it.
3. The owner taps "Mark delivered". The checklist is complete, so the button is active; the thank-you message is
sent.
4. On day 3 and day 30 the Follow-ups due list offers the review request and the check-in.
21.4 A returning customer
 1. The customer signs in and lands on the shop home. The Continue strip shows the order that needs attention.
2. They tap Pay balance, or Order again on a delivered piece (P2).
21.5 Going live after testing
 1. The owner enters payment methods, delivery fees, products, images and templates.
2. The owner places and processes a test order and a test payment end to end.
3. The owner downloads a backup, then runs Clear test data. Order numbers restart.
