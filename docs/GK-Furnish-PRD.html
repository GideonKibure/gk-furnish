Product requirements

GK Furnish

A bilingual workshop storefront and owner portal for furniture made in Mbeya, Tanzania: catalogue, accounts, checkout, PalmPesa mobile money, custom work, and the records the workshop needs to build, deliver and get paid.

1. Purpose

This document states what GK Furnish is for, who it serves, and the behaviour the product must keep. It describes the product as it works in the codebase on 7 November 2026. It is a product document, not a technical design or a marketing brochure.

When a later change conflicts with a requirement here, the change should update this document in the same piece of work.

2. Product summary

GK Furnish is the public shop and private workshop office of a furniture maker in Mbeya. Customers browse chairs, tables, beds, lamps and custom work, choose size, material and colour, and order for delivery or collection. The owner runs products, orders, production, stock, suppliers, money and the words and pictures on the site from one admin portal.

The shop is for a general audience: homes, students, schools, offices, hotels, guest houses, churches and other organisations. It is not a student-only catalogue.

Positioning. Durable furniture, made by hand in Mbeya or with checked partner carpenters, at a price agreed before work starts. Study chairs and study tables are made in the workshop. Beds and larger pieces may be made with partners and are checked before they leave.

2.1 Goals

  • Let a customer choose a real piece, agree the options, and place an order without a phone call.
  • Collect an advance before production, record every payment, and clear the balance before handover.
  • Accept M-Pesa, Airtel Money, Yas (Tigo Pesa) and HaloPesa through PalmPesa, plus cash recorded by the owner.
  • Give the owner one place to see orders, stock, production, custom quotes, expenses and what is still owed.
  • Keep the shop understandable in English and Kiswahili, on a phone and on a desktop.

2.2 Non-goals

  • A marketplace of many independent sellers. There is one workshop and one owner.
  • Staff roles below the owner. The admin portal is for the owner.
  • Collecting a mobile-money PIN on the website. The customer approves the charge on their phone.
  • Treating a payment as successful when PalmPesa has only started it. Only a completed PalmPesa payment adds money.
  • A second payments backend. PalmPesa runs through the existing order and payment records. Snippe remains only as an unused fallback when no PalmPesa token is set.

5. Customer site

Public pages: home, shop, product, categories, cart, checkout, payment waiting, order success, order tracking, gallery, about, process, FAQ, custom order, contact, privacy, account (sign in, register, forgot and reset password), and saved addresses, orders, custom requests and notifications.

5.1 Catalogue

CAT-1The shop lists products with category, price or price range, availability, and a way to like a product.
CAT-2A product can offer materials, sizes and colours. The customer must choose every option the product defines before it can be added to the cart.
CAT-3Price can depend on the material and size. A product may show an old price and an editable discount percent.
CAT-4The owner chooses whether stock is shown as a label or as an exact count.
CAT-5A piece that is unavailable cannot be bought. The cart tells the customer to remove it.
CAT-6Views and likes are shared totals. They use a public database function and an anonymous visitor id stored in the browser. They do not require an account and they do not expose visitor records.
CAT-7Add to cart keeps the customer shopping. Buy now opens checkout on the payment step so the customer can choose how to pay.

5.2 Cart and account

ACC-1A visitor may build a cart before signing in. The cart is kept in the browser.
ACC-2A signed-in customer’s cart is stored on their account and merged with the browser cart when they sign in.
ACC-3Registration is email and password. Google sign-in is supported. Password reset is available.
ACC-4The customer can keep a profile, saved addresses, order history, custom-request history and notifications.
ACC-5Placing an order requires a signed-in customer. The review step shows “Sign in to place order” when there is no session. The server rejects an order that has no account.
ACC-6The owner signs in on the same page. An admin session is sent to the admin portal.

5.3 Checkout

Checkout has three steps: delivery details, payment, and review.

CHK-1The customer gives a name, phone, optional email, the date they need the piece, and optional notes.
CHK-2Fulfilment is delivery or collection. Collection is free, from the workshop address. Delivery asks for region, district and area. The delivery fee is “to be confirmed” unless every item is marked free delivery.
CHK-3The customer chooses an advance or the full amount. The advance percent comes from the tier in section 7.
CHK-4When PalmPesa is configured, the customer chooses mobile money or cash. Mobile money shows four wallets and a phone number for that wallet.
CHK-5When PalmPesa is not configured, checkout explains the offline path (WhatsApp and cash). It does not pretend a phone prompt was sent.
CHK-6The customer must confirm that the design and specifications are agreed, and that later changes must be agreed before production.
CHK-7After a successful mobile-money start, the customer is taken to a private payment page. After a cash order, the order exists and stays unpaid until the owner records the cash.
CHK-8Buy now skips ahead to the payment step. If delivery details are still missing, the customer is returned to that step before review.

5.4 Custom orders

CUS-1A customer can describe a piece that is not in the catalogue, including size, material, colour, finish and a budget range.
CUS-2The owner replies with a quote. The customer can accept, decline, or ask for changes. A newer quote replaces an older one.
CUS-3No production starts on a custom request until the quote is accepted and the advance rules in section 7 are met.

5.5 After the order

ORD-1The customer can open the order from their account and from a private tracking link. The tracking link is unguessable.
ORD-2The order shows status, amounts paid and still due, each payment, and a receipt when a receipt number exists.
ORD-3A paid deposit is shown as “Deposit paid” while a balance remains. The order is not described as fully paid.
ORD-4The customer can pay the rest of the advance or the remaining balance from the order, using the same wallet choice.
ORD-5Only a customer who received the product can submit a review. The review is hidden until the owner approves it. A product can be reviewed once by that customer.

5.6 Content pages

Home, about, process, gallery, FAQ, contact and privacy are part of the product. The owner can replace images used on the home page, shop, categories, gallery, about and custom-order page. Contact and product pages can open WhatsApp with a prepared message. The privacy page is the public privacy policy used for Google sign-in.

6. Payments

PalmPesa is the mobile-money provider. The customer never types a PIN into GK Furnish. PalmPesa sends a prompt to the phone. The customer enters the PIN on that phone. The site marks the payment paid only after PalmPesa reports the payment as completed.

6.1 Wallets

The customer chooses the wallet, then enters the number that belongs to it. PalmPesa itself routes by the phone number. GK Furnish checks that the number’s prefix matches the wallet before sending the charge.

WalletAccepted prefixes after 255 or a leading 0
M-Pesa74, 75, 76
Airtel Money68, 69, 78, 79
Yas (Tigo Pesa)65, 67, 71
HaloPesa61, 62
PAY-1A mismatched number is refused with a clear message and no charge is sent.
PAY-2The waiting page names the wallet and the masked phone, and tells the customer to enter the PIN on the phone prompt.
PAY-3The page keeps checking while the payment is pending. If the prompt expires, it keeps checking for a late completion.
PAY-4The smallest furniture mobile-money charge is 500 TZS. PalmPesa’s own minimum is 100 TZS. The charge never exceeds the balance still due.
PAY-5Cash is not a mobile-money charge. The order stays unpaid until the owner records the cash. Recording the same cash amount and method twice within two minutes is blocked.
PAY-6A failed, cancelled, rejected or expired PalmPesa result does not add money. A later completed result for that same payment still counts as paid, once.
PAY-7The same PalmPesa callback is applied once. An amount that does not match the payment is refused.
PAY-8Each completed payment gets a receipt number. Stock is reduced once, when the advance is first reached, not on every later payment.
PAY-9Someone who does not own the order and does not hold the private payment link cannot pay it.
PAY-10The PalmPesa token stays on the server. It is not placed in the browser, in a public environment variable, or in git.

6.2 How status becomes paid

  1. The site creates a pending payment and asks PalmPesa to start it.
  2. PalmPesa notifies the site, and the site also asks PalmPesa for the current status.
  3. The customer’s payment page, the order page, the admin lists, and a scheduled check all look for payments that are still open.
  4. Only a verified completed status adds the amount to the order.

There is no PalmPesa sandbox. A test charge is a real charge.

The owner can change the percentages and the thresholds. The defaults are:

POL-1Production cannot start until the advance has been received and the owner has marked the design and specifications as agreed.
POL-2Dispatch, delivery and completion also require a confirmed delivery fee, when a fee is still pending, and a zero balance.
POL-3A paid advance does not mean the order is fully paid. The balance is the order total minus completed payments, after refunds.
POL-4Cancelling an order requires a reason. Refunding an order is done from a payment, not by picking a status from a list.
POL-5Any change to the design after the order must be agreed before production, and the agreement is recorded on the order.

7.1 Order statuses

Pending, awaiting payment, confirmed, processing, in production, ready, dispatched, delivered, completed, cancelled, refunded. Custom quotes use their own states: draft, sent, accepted, declined, changes requested, expired, and replaced.

Payment rows use pending, completed, failed and expired. A cancelled or rejected PalmPesa result is stored as failed, with the reason kept.

10. Quality the product must hold

  • A payment page must not show success before PalmPesa has completed the payment.
  • Refreshing the site must not sign the person out.
  • Private order and payment links must be long and random.
  • Payment records are not readable from the public browser database. The server reads and writes them.
  • The PalmPesa token, signing secret and database service key stay on the server.
  • Repeating a PalmPesa notification must not add the money twice.
  • The shop should open quickly. Images and pages should not force sideways scrolling.

12. Main records

  • Product — name, Kiswahili name, category, price, compare-at price, discount, options, colours, stock display, free-delivery flag, source (workshop or partner), availability.
  • Cart line — product, quantity, material, size, colour, and the customer when signed in.
  • Order — customer account, contact, fulfilment, place, needed-by date, notes, totals, advance percent and amount, amount paid, amount still due, payment status, delivery-fee status, specifications-agreed time, status, tracking token.
  • Payment — order, amount, purpose (advance, balance or full), method, status, receipt, PalmPesa transaction and provider ids, wallet, channel, failure reason, paid time.
  • Payment event — PalmPesa event id, used so the same notice is not applied twice.
  • Review — product, verified buyer, rating, text, approval.
  • Custom request and quote — the customer’s brief and the owner’s priced reply.
  • Capital and loan — money put into the business, with borrowed, repaid, interest and still owed.

13. Main journeys

13.1 Buy a catalogue piece

  1. The customer opens a product and chooses colour, size and material where those exist.
  2. Buy now adds the piece and opens checkout on the payment step. Add to cart does not.
  3. They choose a wallet and enter that wallet’s number, or they choose cash.
  4. They complete delivery details and the date, review the order, and confirm the specifications.
  5. If they are not signed in, they sign in or create an account and return to checkout.
  6. For mobile money, PalmPesa prompts the phone. They enter the PIN on the phone. The site shows paid only after completion.
  7. They follow the order until the balance is cleared and the piece is delivered or collected.

13.2 Pay the rest later

  1. From the account order, the customer chooses the wallet and number again.
  2. They can pay what is left of the advance, or the full remaining balance.
  3. The same confirmation rules apply. The owner can also record cash or a bank payment.

13.3 Custom piece

  1. The customer sends a brief.
  2. The owner sends a quote.
  3. The customer accepts, asks for changes, or declines.
  4. After acceptance, the advance and specification rules gate production in the same way as a catalogue order.

15. Open points

These are known gaps. They are not silent requirements.

  • The FAQ still answers “Do I need an account?” with “No”, while placing an order now requires an account. That answer should be corrected.
  • The four wallets appear only after PALMPESA_API_TOKEN is set on the host and the site is redeployed. Without it, checkout shows the offline explanation.
  • Database migration 0010 must be applied so PalmPesa columns and payment events exist. Earlier shop migrations must already be applied.
  • Transport and inspection are not inside the stock unit cost. Purchase-order deposits are display-only. Overdue alerts appear when the dashboard is opened. Product sales reports use order value, not cash received.
  • Loans have no repayment schedule beyond the payments the owner records.
  • There is no staff role below the owner, and admin multi-factor sign-in is not built.
  • A Vercel hobby plan may reject a scheduled check every five minutes. The payment page and the admin lists still check open PalmPesa payments.

16. Glossary

TermMeaning
AdvanceThe portion that must be paid before production starts. Default 50, 60 or 70 percent.
BalanceOrder total minus completed payments, after refunds. This is what is still owed.
Deposit paidThe advance was paid and a balance remains. The order payment status is paid for that deposit, and the screen says deposit paid.
WalletM-Pesa, Airtel Money, Yas (Tigo Pesa) or HaloPesa.
PromptThe phone message from the network that asks for the PIN. It is not a field on the website.
CompletedThe only PalmPesa result that means the money was received.
CollectionThe customer picks the piece up. There is no delivery fee.
Free deliveryA product flag. If every item has it, the delivery fee is zero. Otherwise the owner confirms the fee after the order.
